This page recalls the notions used by the package and fixes its conventions. The references are listed on the page [Bibliography](../bibliography.md).

## Garside monoids

A *Garside monoid* [DP99, DDGKM15] is a cancellative monoid ``M``, generated by its *atoms*, in which divisibility is a lattice order, and which has a *Garside element* ``Δ``: an element whose left and right divisors coincide, are finite in number and generate ``M``. The divisors of ``Δ`` are called the *simples* of ``M``. A Garside monoid ``M`` embeds in its enveloping group ``G(M)``, which is a group of fractions and is called a *Garside group*. The braid group ``B(W)`` of a finite Coxeter group ``W`` is a Garside group in two ways: the classical braid monoid ``B⁺(W)`` and the dual braid monoid ``B^*(W)`` [Bes03] are two submonoids of ``B(W)`` that are Garside monoids and whose enveloping group is ``B(W)``. The braid group ``B(W)`` of a well-generated complex reflection group ``W`` is also a Garside group, with the corresponding dual braid monoid ``B^*(W)`` as Garside monoid.

Conventions: ``≼`` denotes the *left divisibility* relation on simples, that is, ``s ≼ t`` holds when ``t = su`` is satisfied for some ``u``; in that case, the properties of a Garside monoid ensure that ``u`` is a uniquely defined simple, called the *right complement* of ``s`` in ``t`` and denoted by ``s \ t``. If ``s`` and ``t`` are simples, then ``s ∨ t`` denotes, when it is defined, the *least common right multiple* (or *right lcm*) of ``s`` and ``t``. Note that [DL99] and [Gar24], for example, use the opposite conventions, exchanging left and right: the package includes the [`opposite`](@ref) function, to work with the opposite monoid.

## Germs

The package stores everything it needs in the *germ* of a Garside monoid, which contains the simples and operations on them, in particular all the products of simples that are themselves simples. The package uses several concrete types for germs, all of them being subtypes of [`AbstractGerm`](@ref). In a germ, the simples are numbered: `0` is the identity and `1:N` are the atoms. For the user, they are [`GermElement`](@ref)s, written as their smallest word in the atoms: in ``B⁺(A₂)``, the simples are `e`, `1`, `2`, `12`, `21` and ``Δ =`` `121`.

## LCM resolutions

Dehornoy and Lafont associated, to a Garside monoid and to a linear order of its atoms, a free resolution of finite type, the *order resolution*, whose generating cells correspond to certain sequences of atoms that admit an lcm. The package builds a family of resolutions of this kind, the *lcm resolutions*, as free resolutions of the monoid algebra ``ℤM`` by free ``ℤM``-bimodules: their generating cells are some of those of the Garside complex, that is, some chains ``x₁|⋯|xₙ`` of simples, kept or discarded by a choice made at each lcm using an order of type [`LCMOrdering`](@ref):
- with [`TrivialPreorder`](@ref) and an order of the atoms, the construction gives the order resolution of Dehornoy and Lafont for that atom order;
- with [`ComplementDeglex`](@ref), the default, it gives the *reduced* lcm resolution, with fewer generating cells; in the cases checked so far, it is minimal (in the sense that no generating cell can be removed).

Generating cells are written in bar form, with simples as letters: for example, `2|12` in ``B⁺(A₂)``; `e` is the unique generating cell of degree ``0``. The *weight* of a generating cell is the product of its letters, which is a simple by construction, and the boundaries of the lcm resolution preserve the weights. The construction of the lcm resolutions, including the reduced one, is described in an article in preparation.

## Homology

For a Garside monoid ``M``, as for every monoid that satisfies the Ore conditions, the enveloping group ``G(M)`` is a group of fractions, and the homology of ``M`` with coefficients in a ``ℤG(M)``-module ``X`` (seen as a ``ℤM``-module) is that of ``G(M)`` with coefficients in ``X``, see [DDGKM15, Lemma III.3.27] (and [Gar24] for the extension to Garside categories and groupoids). There, the result is stated for trivial coefficients, but the proof applies to every ``ℤG(M)``-module by the flatness of ``ℤG(M)`` over ``ℤM``. Note that the result does not hold for a general monoid.

If `P` is a computed resolution of ``M`` by free ``ℤM``-bimodules, and `X` is a ``ℤM``-module, then `homology(P, X)` returns the homology of the complex ``C =`` `ℤ ⊗ P ⊗ X`. It calls [SparseElementaryDivisors.jl](https://github.com/yvesguiraud/SparseElementaryDivisors.jl) to compute the elementary divisors of each differential of ``C``. Then, writing ``d_n : C_{n+1} → C_n`` the differentials of ``C``, the homology ``H_n(C) = ker d_{n−1} / im d_n`` has a free part of rank ``rank C_n − rank d_n − rank d_{n−1}``, and its torsion is given by the elementary divisors of ``d_n`` greater than ``1``.

If `P` is a computed resolution of ``M`` by free ``ℤM``-bimodules, and `X` is a ``ℤM``-module of finite rank, then `homology(P, X)` returns the homology of the complex ``C =`` `ℤ ⊗ P ⊗ X`, whose terms are free abelian groups of finite rank. It calls [SparseElementaryDivisors.jl](https://github.com/yvesguiraud/SparseElementaryDivisors.jl) to compute the elementary divisors of each differential of ``C``. Writing ``d_n : C_{n+1} \to C_n`` for the differentials of ``C``, ``c_n`` for the rank of ``C_n``, and ``e_1, \dots, e_{k_n}`` for the elementary divisors of ``d_n``, so that ``k_n = \operatorname{rank} d_n``, the homology in degree ``n`` is

```math
H_n(C) = \ker d_{n-1} / \operatorname{im} d_n \;\cong\; \mathbb{Z}^{\,c_n - k_{n-1} - k_n} \oplus \bigoplus_{i=1}^{k_n} \mathbb{Z}/e_i\mathbb{Z},
```

where the elementary divisors equal to ``1`` give trivial summands.

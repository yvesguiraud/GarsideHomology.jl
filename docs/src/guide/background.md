This page recalls the notions used by the package and fixes its conventions. The references are listed on the page [References](../references.md).

## Garside monoids and germs

A *Garside monoid* (Dehornoy and Paris; see *Foundations of Garside Theory* by Dehornoy, Digne, Godelle, Krammer and Michel) is a cancellative monoid, generated by its *atoms*, in which divisibility is a lattice order, and which has a *Garside element* Δ: an element whose left and right divisors coincide, are finite in number and generate the monoid. These divisors are the *simples*. The monoid embeds in its group of fractions, a *Garside group*. The braid groups of the finite Coxeter groups are Garside groups in two ways: through the classical braid monoid B⁺(W) and through the dual braid monoid B*(W) of Bessis; the braid groups of the well-generated complex reflection groups, through their dual braid monoids.

The package reads everything it needs in the *germ* of the monoid: its simples with their products, when the product of two simples is simple ([`AbstractGerm`](@ref)). The simples are numbered: `0` is the identity and `1:N` are the atoms. For the user, they are [`GermElement`](@ref)s, written as their smallest word in the atoms: in B⁺(A₂), the simples are `e`, `1`, `2`, `12`, `21` and `121` = Δ.

Conventions: `≼` is left divisibility (`s ≼ t` when `t = su`), and the lcms are right lcms, `ω ∨ a`, the smallest common right multiple of `ω` and `a`. Dehornoy and Lafont, and Garnier, use the other side: [`opposite`](@ref) gives the germ of the opposite monoid.

## Lcm resolutions

Dehornoy and Lafont associated to a Garside monoid, and to a linear order of its atoms, a free resolution of finite type, the *order resolution*, whose cells are certain sequences of atoms. The package builds a family of resolutions of this kind, the *lcm resolutions*, as free resolutions of the monoid algebra ℤ[M] by free ℤ[M]-bimodules: their cells are among the chains `x₁|⋯|xₙ` of simples of the Garside complex, kept or discarded by a choice made at each lcm ([`LCMOrdering`](@ref)).
- With [`TrivialPreorder`](@ref) and an order of the atoms, the construction gives the order resolution of Dehornoy and Lafont for that order.
- With [`ComplementDeglex`](@ref), the default, it gives the *reduced* resolution, with fewer cells; in the cases checked so far, it is minimal.

A cell is written in bar form, its letters being simples: `2|12` in B⁺(A₂); `e` is the cell of degree 0. Its *weight* is the product of its letters, a simple, and the boundaries preserve the weights. The construction is described in an article in preparation.

## Homology

For a Garside monoid, as for every monoid that satisfies the Ore conditions, the homology of the monoid with coefficients in a module is that of its group of fractions (Squier; Garnier, for categories). For a resolution `R` by bimodules and a module of coefficients `M`, `homology(R, M)` is the homology over ℤ of the complex `ℤ ⊗ R ⊗ M`, computed from the elementary divisors of its differentials by [SparseElementaryDivisors.jl](https://github.com/yvesguiraud/SparseElementaryDivisors.jl).

Conventions:
- The differential `d_n : C_{n+1} → C_n` is indexed by its target, whereas Hecke and most texts index `d_n : C_n → C_{n−1}` by its source. Then `H_n = ker d_{n−1} / im d_n`, of free rank `rank C_n − rank d_n − rank d_{n−1}`, and its torsion is given by the elementary divisors of `d_n` greater than 1.
- B⁺(W) and B*(W) are the classical and dual braid monoids of the reflection group W, and B(W) is its braid group.
- ℤ is the trivial module and ℤε the sign module, in which every atom acts by −1, hence every braid reflection.
- A finitely generated abelian group is written as in Garnier's tables, torsion first: `(ℤ/3ℤ)² ⊕ ℤ/6ℤ ⊕ ℤ`, and `0` for the trivial group.

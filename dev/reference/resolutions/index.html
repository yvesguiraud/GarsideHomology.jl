Resolutions and certificates

Exported: lcm_resolution, certify. The other names are public only.

Resolutions

GarsideHomology.lcm_resolution — Function
lcm_resolution(G::AbstractGerm, ordering::LCMOrdering = LCMOrdering())
lcm_resolution(P::LCMProjectionData)

The lcm resolution of the germ G for the ordering ordering: the free resolution of the monoid algebra ℤ[M] by ℤ[M]-bimodules obtained from the Garside complex by the matching of the cells (see LCMOrdering, CellTree, LCMProjectionData), as an LCMResolution, a chain complex over MonoidAlgebra(ZZ, G, :both).

Its module of degree n has for basis the cells of degree n (CellBasis), and differential(R, n), from degree n + 1 to degree n, is a LabelledSparseMatrix whose column j is the boundary of the j-th cell of degree n + 1, with labels (σ, τ) for the terms coef · σ y τ. Its tensor products by modules of coefficients (homology(R, M), homology(R, ℤ), ℤ ⊗ R ⊗ ℤ, extend_scalars) compute the homology of the monoid, that of its group of fractions.

  • lcm_resolution(G): the reduced resolution (ComplementDeglex).
  • lcm_resolution(G, LCMOrdering(TrivialPreorder(); root_order = p)): the order resolution of Dehornoy and Lafont for the order p of the atoms.
  • With Chevie loaded, lcm_resolution(M, ordering) for a BraidMonoid or a DualBraidMonoid M of Chevie is that of its germ, lcm_resolution(germ(M), ordering) (germ).

The first form drops the memo of the projection at the end. The second one reads the boundaries from data P built by the caller, who keeps its memo.

GarsideHomology.LCMResolution — Type
LCMResolution <: GarsideHomology.Complexes.AbstractChainComplex

The lcm resolution of a germ for an ordering, as returned by lcm_resolution: a chain complex of free bimodules over MonoidAlgebra(ZZ, G, :both), which keeps the tree of its cells (cell_tree), hence the germ and the ordering.

It implements the interface of AbstractChainComplex (range, R[n], differential, base_ring) through the ChainComplex it wraps, whose bases are CellBasises; extend_scalars gives a ChainComplex over ℤ on the same bases, which still know their cells. Functions that need more than a chain complex, such as the writing of snapshots (write_snapshot), have methods for this type.

Its cells, as Cells: basis(R[n]), basis(R) (the GradedBasis), R("2|12") (from the bar form), R(G(2), G(1, 2)) (from the letters); the elements of its modules, R(x), R("2×1[2]1 - [1]") (BasedFreeModuleElement); its differential, d = differential(R), d(x) (Differential). At the REPL, its detailed display gives the cells of each degree and the boundaries d(x), within the screen (the beginning and the end of the list, the boundaries of degree 1, a[] - []a, omitted but for a reminder); show(stdout, "text/plain", R), or the same into a file, writes all of them.

GarsideHomology.CellBasis — Type
CellBasis

The basis of the module of degree n of an lcm resolution: the cells of degree n of its tree, as Cells, in the order of the construction (the numbers cell_range(T, n)), created on demand. A vector that keeps the tree, so that a complex built on such bases, and its extensions of scalars, which share them, know their cells (cell_tree). At the REPL, a vector of cells in bar form, cut in the middle when it is long.

Certificates

GarsideHomology.certify — Function
certify(R::LCMResolution; square_zero = true, mobius = :auto,
        nworkers = Threads.nthreads()) -> ResolutionCertificate

Check the lcm resolution R (lcm_resolution) and return the report, a ResolutionCertificate; a failed check is a field of the report, never an exception.

  • Homogeneity, terms 1·y·1 and lengths of the labels: one pass over the terms.
  • ∂² = 0 in the bimodules: each column of d_{n+1} composed with the columns of d_n, the products of simples read in the germ, then put in normal form. With nworkers > 1 and a germ safe for concurrent reads (a TableGerm), the columns are split between nworkers tasks; a lazy germ stays on one thread. square_zero = false skips this check, the costliest.
  • The Möbius function μ(1, ω), for the Euler characteristic and the degrees by weight: mobius = :crosscut by the crosscut theorem of Rota (2^N lcms, any germ, at most 24 atoms), :layers by a pass over the intervals (a germ that lists its simples), :none to skip; :auto takes the crosscut up to 16 atoms (the braid monoids of Coxeter groups, even lazy), the layers above (the dual braid monoids).

The arithmetic of the coefficients is checked: an overflow throws an OverflowError rather than a wrong report.

GarsideHomology.ResolutionCertificate — Type
ResolutionCertificate

The report of certify on a resolution of bimodules over a germ. Each check gives a number of failures, 0 when it passes, or nothing when it was not done.

Correctness, which must hold for every ordering, minimal or not:

  • homogeneity: the terms σ y τ of a boundary ∂x with σ·π(y)·τ ≠ π(x), π being the weight of a cell;
  • square_zero: the columns of d_n ∘ d_{n+1} that are not zero, computed in the bimodules with the products of the germ (nothing with square_zero = false);
  • euler: the simples ω where the Euler characteristic of the cells of weight ω, Σₙ (−1)ⁿ cₙ(ω), differs from μ(1, ω) (nothing without the Möbius function).

Properties, never failures of the resolution:

  • identity_terms[n + 1]: the terms 1·y·1 of d_n, which a minimal resolution has none;
  • split_weights: the weights whose cells lie in several degrees (nothing without μ); with the Euler characteristic, none means cₙ(ω) = |μ(1, ω)| in a single degree;
  • mobius_sum: Σ_ω |μ(1, ω)|, the number of cells of such a resolution;
  • max_label_length[n + 1]: the largest ℓ(σ) + ℓ(τ) over the terms of d_n (0 if there are none).

Also: cells[n + 1] and terms[n + 1], the numbers of cells of degree n and of terms of d_n; mobius_method, how μ was computed (:crosscut, :layers or :none); failures, the first failure of each failed check, in words (degree, cell named by its atom sequence as in the snapshots, term or weight); times, the seconds of each step.

Read it with is_correct, is_minimal and is_linear.

GarsideHomology.is_correct — Function
is_correct(c::ResolutionCertificate) -> Bool

Whether the three checks of correctness were done and passed: homogeneity, ∂² = 0 and the Euler characteristic by weight. A check that was not done gives false: the resolution is then not certified.

is_minimal(c::ResolutionCertificate) -> Bool

Whether the differential has no term 1·y·1: the criterion of minimality of a resolution graded by the weight.

GarsideHomology.is_linear — Function
is_linear(c::ResolutionCertificate) -> Bool

Whether every term σ y τ of the differential has ℓ(σ) + ℓ(τ) = 1: no term 1·y·1 and at most 1 in each degree.

For developers

GarsideHomology.write_snapshot — Function
write_snapshot(path, R::LCMResolution; case = "", extra = Pair{String, String}[],
               fingerprints = true, germ_fingerprint = :compute)

Write the lcm resolution R (lcm_resolution) as a snapshot of format version 1, gzip-compressed, at path, which must end with .txt.gz: the header, then the lines of the cells by increasing degree and those of the differential by increasing n, sorted by bytes within each group (test/data/reference/FORMAT.md). With fingerprints = true, also write the fingerprints at the same place, with the extension .sha.csv (FORMAT.md, section 5). For another complex, an ArgumentError.

The header gives the format, case (if not empty), the ordering, the side convention, the number of atoms and the fingerprint of the germ (fingerprint), then the pairs key => value of extra (what only the caller knows: label, construction, machine, commits), then the versions of GarsideHomology and Julia, the date and the numbers of cells and terms (FORMAT.md, section 7). It does not enter the fingerprints.

The fingerprint of the germ can cost most of the writing for a large tabulated germ (such as B⁺(A₈), whose 362 880 simples are tabulated): germ_fingerprint takes it already computed (a GermFingerprint or a string), for instance once for the snapshots of several orderings of the same germ, or nothing to omit it; by default (:compute), it is computed if the germ lists its simples, and omitted for a lazy germ.

Cells

The cells of an lcm resolution are the nodes of a tree, numbered by integers; a Cell carries a number together with its tree.

Exported: ncells, cell_weight, cell_degree, atom_sequence. The other names are public only.

Cells

GarsideHomology.Cell — Type
Cell

A cell of an lcm resolution, for the user: the tree of the cells (CellTree) and the number of the cell in it. It is written in bar form, x₁|⋯|xₙ, its letters being written as words in the atoms (GermElement), and e for the cell of degree 0, following the settings of the display (Notation): 2|12 in B⁺(A₂).

The elements of the bases of the modules of a resolution (basis(P[n]), a CellBasis), and of its extensions of scalars. A cell is also given by its bar form, P("2|12"), or by its letters, P(G(2), G(1, 2)).

A cell reads as the sequence of its letters: c[i] is the i-th letter (a GermElement), c[end] the last one, length(c) the degree, and collect(c) and the iteration give the letters. Also cell_weight(c) (the product of the letters), cell_degree(c) and atom_sequence(c) (the atoms that created the cell, its name in the snapshots). Two cells are equal if they are the same cell of the same tree; isless is the order of the construction. A Cell is a scalar for broadcasting.

Tree of the cells

GarsideHomology.CellTree — Type
CellTree(G::AbstractGerm, ordering::LCMOrdering = LCMOrdering())

The cells of the lcm resolution of the germ G for the ordering ordering, as a tree whose nodes are the cells. The node of a cell x = [τ₁|…|τₙ] has for parent the cell [τ₁|…|τₙ₋₁], for letter its last letter τₙ, for atom the atom a that created it (τₙ = ω \ (ω ∨ a), ω being the weight of the parent), for weight τ₁⋯τₙ and for degree n. The node 1 is the cell of degree 0, of weight the identity.

The tree is built breadth first, by the rule of LCMOrdering: at a node of weight ω whose list I of admissible atoms is in the inherited order (the order of the root at the root), I is sorted by the preorder at ω, ties keeping the inherited order; an atom j is kept if no atom placed before it divides ω ∨ aⱼ; the atoms kept give the children, in that order, and the list of a child is formed of the atoms of its elder siblings. No list is copied: it is read in the tree itself.

The cells are numbered 1:ncells(T) in the order of the construction: by degree, then by parent, then in the order given by the ordering. The children of a node are contiguous, and the cells of degree n form cell_range(T, n), which fixes the order of the basis of the free module of degree n.

Accessors, on the numbers of the cells: ncells, cell_weight, cell_degree and atom_sequence (exported, since they also read a Cell, the cell for the user), cell_range, cell_letter, cell_atom, cell_children, admissible_atoms and bar_form (public, not exported).

GarsideHomology.ChainTree — Type
ChainTree(T::CellTree)

The prefix tree of the chains [x₁|…|xₙ] of the Garside complex visited by the projection of a resolution by lcms: the node of a chain has for parent the chain [x₁|…|xₙ₋₁], for letter xₙ (a simple other than the identity) and for degree n. Its first nodes are the cells of T, with the same numbers, so that a chain is a cell if and only if its number is at most ncells(T); the other chains are added as they are met. The child of a node by a letter is found in a dictionary, whose key packs the number of the node and the letter in a UInt64.

GarsideHomology.LCMProjectionData — Type
LCMProjectionData(T::CellTree)

The data of the projection p of the Garside complex onto the cells of T, which lcm_projection! computes and memoizes: the tree of the cells, the tree of the chains visited (ChainTree), and the memo, the normal form of p(x) for each chain x visited, stored as an interval of a single arena of BimoduleTerms. The boundary of the cells, lcm_boundary, is read from it.

The projection follows the matching of lcm_splitting: p(x) = x for a cell; for x = h|σ with h a cell, p(x) = 0 if x is collapsible and, if x is redundant, matched with ϕx = h|y|z of degree n + 1, p(x) = (−1)ⁿ⁺¹ Σᵢ (−1)ⁱ σᵢ p(dᵢϕx) τᵢ, the sum over the faces of ϕx but the n-th, which is x; for x = h|σ with h not a cell, p(x) = Σ λ s p(c | t·σ) over the terms λ s [c] t of p(h).

GarsideHomology.lcm_splitting — Function
lcm_splitting(T::CellTree, h, σ) -> (y, z)

The splitting of the last letter of the chain h|σ, for a cell h of weight ω and a simple σ ≠ 1 with ωσ simple: σ = y·z, where y is the letter of the first child of h, in the order given by the ordering, whose atom divides ωσ (so that ωy = ω ∨ a), and (0, σ) (the identity for y) if no child of h has an atom dividing ωσ.

It classifies the chain x = h|σ: if y = σ, x is the corresponding child of h, a cell; if y is the identity, x is collapsible and its projection is zero; otherwise x is redundant, matched with the chain h|y|z of degree one more. The filtfact of the preliminary implementation.

GarsideHomology.lcm_projection! — Function
lcm_projection!(P::LCMProjectionData, x) -> AbstractVector{BimoduleTerm}

The projection p(x) of the chain x of the Garside complex onto the cells, in normal form (sorted, equal terms summed, no zero): its terms are cells of the degree of x, with their left and right simples. The chain x is a node of P.chains or the list of its letters. The result is a view of the memo, valid until the next call; the memo and the tree of the chains grow on the way.

GarsideHomology.lcm_boundary — Function
lcm_boundary(P::LCMProjectionData, x) -> Vector{BimoduleTerm}

The boundary of the cell x of degree n ≥ 1 in the resolution: the projection of its boundary in the Garside complex, ∂x = Σᵢ (−1)ⁱ σᵢ p(dᵢx) τᵢ, a combination of cells of degree n − 1 in normal form; empty for the cell of degree 0. The column of x in the matrix of the differential.

Coefficients and homology

Exported: TrivialModule, SignModule, ℤ, ℤε, ℤϵ, homology. Public only: AbstractMonoidModule, tensor_product, ⊗, and ZZ, the ring of integers of AbstractAlgebra, the same object as ℤ. ZZ is not exported because Nemo, Hecke and Oscar export a ZZ of their own; with Hecke or Oscar loaded, homology and differential are to be written GarsideHomology.homology and GarsideHomology.differential, since they also export functions with these names. With Chevie loaded, ⊗ is written without qualification: the extension adds methods to the ⊗ of Chevie.

Modules of coefficients

GarsideHomology.AbstractMonoidModule — Type
AbstractMonoidModule{R}

Supertype of the modules of coefficients: a module over the monoid algebra R[M] of a monoid M, free of finite rank over the ring R (an AbstractAlgebra ring), given by the action of the atoms; a simple acts as a word of atoms. A module does not depend on a germ: the tensor products (tensor_product) compute the action on the simples of the germ of the complex (and check, when needed, that it is well defined).

Interface: base_ring(M), the ring R, and rank(M), the rank over R. A module of rank one is given by a character χ of the monoid, and serves on both sides, s·m = m·s = χ(s)·m.

source
GarsideHomology.TrivialModule — Type
TrivialModule(R = GarsideHomology.ZZ)

The ring R, every element of the monoid acting by 1: the trivial module. The homology with coefficients in TrivialModule() is the homology of the group of fractions with trivial integer coefficients. By default, R is the ring of integers of AbstractAlgebra (GarsideHomology.ZZ), that of the coefficients of the resolutions.

source
GarsideHomology.SignModule — Type
SignModule(R = GarsideHomology.ZZ)

The ring R, every atom acting by −1, so that a simple s acts by (−1)^ℓ(s): for a braid group and the integers, the module ℤε in which the braid reflections act by −1 (Garnier, table 4). Well defined on every homogeneous germ. By default, R is the ring of integers of AbstractAlgebra (GarsideHomology.ZZ).

source
GarsideHomology.ℤε — Constant
ℤε

The sign module over the integers, SignModule(): every atom acts by −1, so that homology(R, ℤε) is the homology with coefficients in ℤε (Garnier, table 4), beside homology(R, ℤ) for the trivial coefficients, and ℤ ⊗ R ⊗ ℤε the complex that computes it. Typed \bbZ then Tab, then \varepsilon then Tab (ε, U+03B5). The other epsilon, \epsilon (ϵ, U+03F5, another character), gives ℤϵ, the same object.

source
GarsideHomology.ℤϵ — Constant
ℤϵ

The same object as ℤε, the sign module over the integers, under the name typed with \epsilon (ϵ, U+03F5) rather than \varepsilon (ε, U+03B5), two characters that look alike.

Rings

GarsideHomology.ℤ — Constant
ℤ

The ring of integers of AbstractAlgebra, that of the coefficients of the resolutions, under a name of its own: the same object as GarsideHomology.ZZ. A ring stands for its trivial module: homology(R, ℤ), ℤ ⊗ R ⊗ ℤ. Typed \bbZ then Tab, in the Julia REPL and in the editors that know Julia (VS Code, Jupyter, Pluto, the Julia modes of Emacs and Vim).

ZZ is public but not exported: Nemo, Hecke and Oscar export a ZZ of their own, another object, and exporting ours would make ZZ ambiguous, hence unusable, for their users. To write ZZ, import it: using GarsideHomology: ZZ (rather than GarsideHomology.ZZ at each use).

Tensor products

GarsideHomology.tensor_product — Function
GarsideHomology.tensor_product(N, C)
GarsideHomology.tensor_product(C, M)
GarsideHomology.tensor_product(N, C, M)
N ⊗ C,  C ⊗ M                  (with `using GarsideHomology: ⊗`, or with Chevie loaded)

The tensor products over the monoid algebra of a complex C of free modules over a MonoidAlgebra by a module of coefficients N on the left (as a right module) or M on the right (as a left module), AbstractMonoidModules over the ring of the coefficients of C (ℤ for now). A ring stands for its trivial module: ℤ ⊗ C ⊗ ℤ is TrivialModule() ⊗ C ⊗ TrivialModule(), that is extend_scalars(C).

  • C of bimodules (an lcm resolution): N ⊗ C is the complex of free right modules whose terms c·σ y τ become χ_N(σ)·c · y τ (labels τ, side :right); C ⊗ M that of free left modules, χ_M(τ)·c · σ y (labels σ, side :left); in each column, the terms of the same row and label are summed. tensor_product(N, C, M), or (N ⊗ C) ⊗ M, is the complex over the ring, χ_N(σ)·χ_M(τ)·c · y.
  • C of right modules (N ⊗ R): C ⊗ M is the complex over the ring; of left modules: N ⊗ C. Tensoring a side that is already tensored is an ArgumentError.

The results share the bases of C, which keep their cells. homology(R, M) is the homology of TrivialModule ⊗ R ⊗ M, that is H₍*₎(B; M) for the monoid B (the homology of its group of fractions). tensor_product is exported by Hecke and Oscar, ⊗ by Hecke, Oscar and Chevie (that of FinitePosets): neither is exported by GarsideHomology. With Chevie loaded, the ⊗ of Chevie has the same methods for our complexes (package extension), so that ℤ ⊗ R ⊗ ℤ works without importing ours.

source
GarsideHomology.:⊗ — Function
⊗(N, C), ⊗(C, M)

The tensor products GarsideHomology.tensor_product(N, C) and GarsideHomology.tensor_product(C, M), N and M modules of coefficients or rings (for their trivial modules). Public, not exported: write using GarsideHomology: ⊗, which wins over the exports of ⊗ by Hecke, Oscar and Chevie. With Chevie loaded, the ⊗ of Chevie (that of FinitePosets) has the same methods for our complexes, and ℤ ⊗ R ⊗ ℤ works without the import.

Homology

All the methods of homology are here: of a chain complex over ℤ, of a resolution with coefficients, and of a germ.

GarsideHomology.Complexes.homology — Function
homology(C::AbstractChainComplex)
homology(C::AbstractChainComplex, n)

Homology of a complex over ℤ: a FinitelyGeneratedGradedAbelianGroup over the degrees of C, or the FinitelyGeneratedAbelianGroup H_n alone. With d_n : C_{n+1} → C_n, the free rank of H_n is rank(C_n) − rank(d_n) − rank(d_{n-1}) and its torsion is given by the elementary divisors of d_n other than 1, computed by SparseElementaryDivisors.sparse_elementary_divisors, to which the keyword arguments are passed (value_type, nworkers, core_format, verbose, ...). homology(C) computes each differential once. A complex over a monoid algebra needs coefficients first: for a resolution R of GarsideHomology, homology(R, M) with a module or a ring (homology(R, ℤ)), or the homology of an extension of scalars, homology(extend_scalars(C)).

source
homology(R::AbstractChainComplex, M; kwargs...)
homology(R::AbstractChainComplex, M, n; kwargs...)

The homology of the monoid, that of its group of fractions, with coefficients in the module M, from a resolution R of bimodules (an lcm resolution): the homology over the ring of M of TrivialModule ⊗ R ⊗ M (tensor_product), in all degrees or in degree n. For a complex of right modules (TrivialModule ⊗ R), the homology of C ⊗ M. M is a module of coefficients, or a ring for its trivial module: homology(R, ℤ) is homology(R, TrivialModule()). The keyword arguments go to the solver, as for homology(C). Without coefficients, homology(R) is an error: the homology of the resolution itself is not that of the monoid.

source
homology(G::AbstractGerm, M = TrivialModule(); ordering = LCMOrdering(), kwargs...)
homology(G::AbstractGerm, M, n; ordering = LCMOrdering(), kwargs...)
homology(G::AbstractGerm, n; ordering = LCMOrdering(), kwargs...)

The homology of the monoid of the germ G, that of its group of fractions, with coefficients in the module M (trivial by default; a ring stands for its trivial module, as in homology(G, ℤ)), in all degrees or in degree n: that of its lcm resolution for ordering, homology(lcm_resolution(G, ordering), M). The resolution is built at each call: to compute several homologies of the same monoid, build it once with lcm_resolution. The other keyword arguments go to the solver, as for homology(C).

With Chevie loaded, the same forms for a BraidMonoid or a DualBraidMonoid of Chevie, through its germ (germ): homology(BraidMonoid(coxgroup(:A, 2))) is the homology of the braid group on three strands.

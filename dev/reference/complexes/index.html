GarsideHomology.Complexes — Module
GarsideHomology.Complexes

Free modules with a basis, labelled sparse matrices, chain complexes and their homology.

The rings of coefficients are AbstractAlgebra rings (AbstractAlgebra.ZZ for now) and monoid algebras described by MonoidAlgebra. A complex over a monoid algebra has LabelledSparseMatrix maps; a complex over ℤ has SparseMatrixCSC maps. The homology of a complex over ℤ, computed by SparseElementaryDivisors, is a FinitelyGeneratedGradedAbelianGroup of FinitelyGeneratedAbelianGroups.

This submodule depends on nothing else in GarsideHomology, and its tests build no monoid, so that it can later become a separate package.

source

The exported names of GarsideHomology.Complexes are also exported by GarsideHomology. Among its public names, rank, basis, MonoidAlgebra, GradedBasis, BasedFreeModuleElement and Differential are also public in GarsideHomology; the others are written GarsideHomology.Complexes.AbstractChainComplex, for instance. With Chevie loaded, basis and rank are written without qualification: the extension adds methods to the functions of Chevie that have these names.

Rings and monoid algebras

GarsideHomology.Complexes.AbstractMonoid — Type
AbstractMonoid

Supertype of the objects that describe a monoid for a MonoidAlgebra: the germs of the GarsideHomology layer, a table of simples read from a file, etc. Such an object fixes the numbering of the elements used as labels (0 for the identity). No method is required by this layer.

source
GarsideHomology.Complexes.MonoidAlgebra — Type
MonoidAlgebra(base, monoid, side = :both)

Descriptor of the monoid algebra base[M] of a monoid M (side = :left or :right), or of the algebra base[M × Mᵒᵖ] over which bimodules are left modules (side = :both). base is an AbstractAlgebra ring and monoid an AbstractMonoid.

Elements of M are encoded by nonnegative integers in the numbering fixed by monoid, 0 being the identity; for side = :both, an element (σ, τ) of M × Mᵒᵖ is encoded by a pair of such integers. These codes are the labels of a LabelledSparseMatrix (see label_type).

base_ring(A) is the ring of coefficients base.

Labelled sparse matrices

GarsideHomology.Complexes.LabelledSparseMatrix — Type
LabelledSparseMatrix{L, Tv}

Sparse m × n matrix over a monoid algebra (MonoidAlgebra). Writing the matrix as D = Σ_γ D_γ · γ, with integer matrices D_γ and γ in the monoid, all the D_γ are stored in a single CSC structure, each stored integer carrying its γ, its label. Column j is the image of the j-th basis element of the source; its stored entry k is the monomial nzval[k] · label[k] in row rowval[k], and the coefficient in row i is the sum of the monomials of column j in row i. A label is the code of an element of the monoid (Int32) or of a pair (σ, τ) for a bimodule (NTuple{2, Int32}), so that a term of a bimodule differential reads c · σ y τ.

Invariants, checked by the constructor unless check = false (see check_invariants):

  1. length(colptr) == n + 1, colptr[1] == 1, colptr is nondecreasing and colptr[n + 1] - 1 is the common length of rowval, label and nzval;
  2. 1 ≤ rowval[k] ≤ m;
  3. in each column, the pairs (rowval[k], label[k]) are strictly increasing: the storage is canonical, two equal matrices have equal arrays;
  4. no stored coefficient is zero;
  5. labels are nonnegative, label 0 (or (0, 0)) being the identity.

Constructors: LabelledSparseMatrix(I, J, L, V, m, n) from a list of monomials (see below), LabelledSparseMatrix(m, n, colptr, rowval, label, nzval; check = true) from the CSC arrays, and LabelledSparseMatrix{L, Tv}(m, n) for the zero matrix; the first two differ by their first argument, a vector of rows or a number of rows. Accessors follow SparseArrays: size, nrows, ncols, nnz, nzrange, rowvals, nonzeros, plus labels.

GarsideHomology.Complexes.extend_scalars — Function
extend_scalars(d::LabelledSparseMatrix)
extend_scalars(d::LabelledSparseMatrix, χ)

The integer matrix obtained by extending the scalars along the character χ of the monoid (of M × M^op for a bimodule), trivial if omitted: each label γ is sent to the integer χ(γ), or to 1, and the monomials of a given row and column are then summed, whatever their labels (trivial module on both sides). One pass over the stored entries; the result is a SparseMatrixCSC{Tv, Int} without stored zeros, computed with checked arithmetic for machine integers. χ is any callable, for instance (σ, τ) ↦ (−1)^ℓ(τ), the sign module on the right (ℤε), with lengths given by the germ. The sign on both sides, (σ, τ) ↦ (−1)^(ℓ(σ) + ℓ(τ)), only changes the signs of the basis elements of a homogeneous resolution (ℓ(σ) + ℓ(τ) is the difference of the lengths of the weights of the source and of the target), hence gives the trivial homology. For a resolution, the typed interface is homology(R, M) and GarsideHomology.tensor_product, with a module of coefficients M or a ring for its trivial module.

source
extend_scalars(C::AbstractChainComplex)
extend_scalars(C::AbstractChainComplex, χ)

The extension of scalars of a complex C over a monoid algebra along the character χ of the monoid (of M × M^op for a complex of bimodules), trivial if omitted: for a complex of bimodules and the trivial character, ℤ ⊗_ℤM C ⊗_ℤM ℤ. The result is the ChainComplex over the ring of coefficients of the monoid algebra, with the same bases (shared, not copied) and the maps extend_scalars(d_n) or extend_scalars(d_n, χ). For a resolution of GarsideHomology, the same with modules of coefficients: homology(R, M), GarsideHomology.tensor_product and ⊗, which also accept a ring for its trivial module (homology(R, ℤ), ℤ ⊗ R ⊗ ℤ).

Chain complexes

The docstrings of AbstractChainComplex include the methods that build the elements of a complex: C("…") from their written form, and, for a resolution, P(x) from a cell and P(G(2), G(1, 2)) from germ elements.

GarsideHomology.Complexes.AbstractChainComplex — Type
AbstractChainComplex{R, D}

Supertype of the chain complexes C_lo ← C_{lo+1} ← … ← C_hi of this layer, over a ring described by an object of type R (an AbstractAlgebra ring or a MonoidAlgebra), with maps of type D (SparseMatrixCSC over ℤ, LabelledSparseMatrix over a monoid algebra). Convention of GarsideHomology: d_n : C_{n+1} → C_n has the index of its target.

Interface, required: range(C) (the degrees lo:hi), C[n] (the module C_n, a BoundsError outside the degrees), differential(C, n) (the map d_n, the zero map outside lo:(hi - 1)) and base_ring(C). Derived, for every subtype: firstindex, lastindex, extend_scalars, is_square_zero, homology, collapsible_pairs. ChainComplex is the generic concrete type; a type that carries more structure (the lcm resolutions of GarsideHomology, for instance) is a subtype of its own, usually wrapping a ChainComplex.

source
GarsideHomology.Complexes.ChainComplex — Type
ChainComplex(modules, maps; lo = 0, check = true)
ChainComplex(maps::AbstractVector{<:SparseMatrixCSC}; lo = 0)

Chain complex C_lo ← C_{lo+1} ← … ← C_hi of free modules over a common ring, where maps[k] is d_n : C_{n+1} → C_n for n = lo + k - 1 (convention of GarsideHomology: d_n has target C_n, whereas Hecke and most texts index d_n : C_n → C_{n-1} by the source). The maps are LabelledSparseMatrixs over a MonoidAlgebra, or SparseMatrixCSCs over ℤ. The second form takes integer matrices only and builds the modules BasedFreeModule(ZZ, r) of the ranks they imply.

Checked: one map fewer than modules, a common ring, size(d_n) == (rank(C_n), rank(C_{n+1})). Not checked: d_n ∘ d_{n+1} = 0 (see is_square_zero).

Accessors: range(C) (degrees lo:hi), C[n] (the module C_n), differential(C, n) (the map d_n), base_ring(C).

GarsideHomology.Complexes.collapsible_pairs — Function
collapsible_pairs(C::AbstractChainComplex)

The collapsible pairs of C: the triples (n, source, target) such that the coefficient of the target-th basis element of C_n in the image of the source-th basis element of C_{n+1} by d_n is a unit, listed by increasing n, then source, then target. The number of pairs is the length of the list, the numbers of distinct sources and targets those of the distinct pairs (n, source) and (n, target).

  • Over a monoid algebra (coefficients in ℤ): the coefficient must be a single monomial, with the label of the identity and the coefficient ±1. A monomial ±γ is a unit only if γ is invertible, that is the identity in a Garside monoid; coefficients with several monomials are excluded, which loses nothing for homogeneous resolutions, where all the monomials of a coefficient have the same total length.
  • Over ℤ: the entry must be ±1. After an extension of scalars this finds many more pairs than in the resolution itself: it is a different notion, useful for the reduction of integer matrices rather than of resolutions.

Graded bases and elements

GarsideHomology.Complexes.GradedBasis — Type
GradedBasis

The graded basis of a chain complex C, as returned by basis(C): X[n] is the basis of C_n, basis(C[n]), for n in range(X), the degrees of C (a BoundsError outside them); X[end], length(X) (the number of degrees), pairs(X) (degree and basis) and the iteration over the bases, by increasing degree. At the REPL, one line per degree, cut in the middle when it is too long; show(io, X) gives the summary line.

source
GarsideHomology.Complexes.BasedFreeModuleElement — Type
BasedFreeModuleElement

An element of a free module with a basis M (BasedFreeModule): a finite sum of terms c×σ[x]τ, with x an element of the basis, c an integer and, over a monoid algebra, σ and τ elements of the monoid on the sides of the module (both for bimodules, one for left or right modules, none over a ring). Kept as sorted vectors of indices, labels and coefficients (Int, with checked arithmetic), without zeros, so that equality is that of the vectors. The terms are in the order of the basis, then, for bimodules, of τ, then of σ: the terms σ[x] come before the terms [x]τ.

Built by calling the module: M(x), the generator [x] for an element x of the basis (a Cell for a resolution); M("2×12[1|21]3 - [2|12]"), read from its written form (a text without brackets is a generator, M("2|12")); over a ring, M(v) from the vector of its coefficients on the basis; zero(M). For a complex, C(x) for a cell and C("…") find the module themselves.

Operations: +, -, multiples by integers, the actions of the elements of the monoid on the sides of the module (σ * v, v * τ, with GermElements; an ArgumentError if a product is not a simple), equality and hashing, iszero, parent(v) (the module); v[x] (over a ring), v[σ, x], v[x, τ] or v[σ, x, τ], the coefficient of a term; the iteration over the terms, as tuples (c, σ, x, τ) (nothing for an absent side); length(v), the number of terms. Over a ring, Vector(v) and SparseVector(v) give the coefficients on the basis, of length rank(M).

Written as 2×12[1|21]3 - [2|12], following the settings of the display (Notation): the identity is omitted on both sides, the coefficients ±1 too, the cell of degree 0 is [], and a marker × separates a coefficient from what follows when the elements of the monoid are written with digits.

source
GarsideHomology.Complexes.Differential — Type
Differential

The differential d of a chain complex C as a whole, as returned by differential(C): d(x), or d[x], is the boundary of the generator x of C_{n+1} (a Cell of a resolution, which knows its degree), and d(v) that of an element v of a module of C, an element of C_n. For the terms c×σ[x]τ of a module over a monoid algebra, d(v) is the sum of the c × σ·d(x)·τ, an ArgumentError if a product of elements of the monoid is not a simple. The boundary of an element of the lowest degree is 0, in a module of rank 0 below it (whose elements have the boundary 0 too). d(d(x)) checks d² = 0 on x. At the REPL, d shows the boundaries of the generators, within the screen; d.(basis(C[n])) gives those of one degree.

Display

GarsideHomology.Complexes.Notation — Type
Notation(; atom_names = nothing, atom_separator = :auto, identity = "e",
         garside_symbol = nothing, bar_separator = "|", empty_cell = "e",
         empty_cell_in_module = "[]", coefficient_style = :auto,
         differential_symbol = "d")
Notation(N::Notation; settings...)

The settings of the display of the elements of a monoid (a simple is written as a word in the atoms), of the cells (x₁|⋯|xₙ) and of the elements of the free modules (c×σ[x]τ). The second form copies N with the settings given changed. The settings of the session are changed by set_notation! and read by notation; for one display, give a Notation under the key :notation of an IOContext, which wins over the session: show(IOContext(stdout, :notation => Notation(atom_separator = ".")), x).

  • atom_names: the names of the atoms 1:N. nothing, their numbers; :subscripts, a₁, a₂, …; a vector of strings (the atoms beyond its length keep their numbers); or a function of the number of an atom.
  • atom_separator: written between the atoms of a word. :auto writes them side by side when every name of an atom of the germ is a single character, possibly followed by subscript digits or primes (1 to 9, a, s₁₂, b′), and separates them by · otherwise, in the whole germ (germs with at least 10 atoms numbered); a string imposes the separator.
  • identity: the identity of the monoid.
  • garside_symbol: nothing writes the Garside element as a word; a string ("Δ", "δ") writes it by that symbol, for a germ that has one.
  • bar_separator, empty_cell, empty_cell_in_module: the separator of the letters of a cell, the cell of degree 0, and its image [e] in a free module.
  • coefficient_style: how an integer coefficient other than ±1 is separated from what follows it. :auto writes × only when the names of the atoms can begin with a digit, hence for the numbered atoms (2×12[1|21]3, 2×[x]), and nothing otherwise (2ab[x]c); :times (×) and :star (*) always; :parentheses puts the monomial in parentheses (2(12[1|21]3)).
  • differential_symbol: the name of the differential in the displays of complexes.

The reading of expressions follows the same settings.

Finitely generated modules

GarsideHomology.Complexes.FinitelyGeneratedModule — Type
FinitelyGeneratedModule{T}(rank, torsion = T[])
FinitelyGeneratedAbelianGroup(rank, torsion = BigInt[])

Finitely generated module R^rank ⊕ R/(t₁) ⊕ … ⊕ R/(tₖ) over a principal ideal domain R, stored by its free rank and its invariant factors t₁ | t₂ | … | tₖ, none of them a unit; T is the type of the invariant factors. Only T = BigInt is implemented for now, that is R = ℤ, under the alias FinitelyGeneratedAbelianGroup; polynomial factors, for k[t, t⁻¹], will follow.

Over ℤ the constructor accepts any list of positive integers: factors equal to 1 are dropped and the others are brought to invariant-factor form, so that ℤ/2ℤ ⊕ ℤ/3ℤ becomes ℤ/6ℤ. Displayed as in Garnier's tables, torsion first: (ℤ/3ℤ)² ⊕ ℤ/6ℤ ⊕ ℤ, and 0 for the trivial group. parse(FinitelyGeneratedAbelianGroup, s) reads this form, and also ℤ/2, Z for ℤ, ^k for an exponent and + for ⊕.

rank(H) is the free rank, torsion(H) the invariant factors.

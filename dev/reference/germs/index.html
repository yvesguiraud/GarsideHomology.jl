Germs

A germ gives the simples of a Garside monoid, numbered by integers, with their products and their left complements.

Exported: natoms, nsimples, simples, opposite, germ, dual_germ. The other names are public only. With Chevie loaded, word and elements are written without qualification: the extension adds methods to the functions of Chevie that have these names.

Interface of a germ

GarsideHomology.AbstractGerm — Type
AbstractGerm <: GarsideHomology.Complexes.AbstractMonoid

Supertype of the germs of Garside monoids, in the sense of Foundations of Garside Theory (Dehornoy, Digne, Godelle, Krammer, Michel, ch. VI): the simples with their partial product, from which the construction of the resolution reads everything it needs. A germ is the descriptor of the monoid of a MonoidAlgebra: it fixes the numbering of the simples, which are the labels of the differentials.

Numbering

Simples are numbered by Int32: 0 is the identity and 1:N are the atoms, in the order of the germ (for a germ built from Chevie, the order of B.atoms), so that the atom i is the simple i. A tabulated germ numbers all its simples in the canonical order, by length, then by smallest word (lexicographic on the atoms); its last simple is then Δ. A lazy germ numbers its simples as it meets them, after the identity and the atoms. The order of the atoms is part of the germ.

Contract

  • Homogeneity: atoms have length 1, every reduced word of a simple s has ℓ(s) letters, and ℓ(st) = ℓ(s) + ℓ(t) when st is simple.
  • Bounded Garside germ: for a simple ω and an atom a, the right lcm ω ∨ a exists and is simple; if s ≼ t are simples, then s \ t is simple; the simples are the divisors of Δ.
  • Side: ≼ is left divisibility and lcms are right lcms (ω ≼ ω ∨ a), the convention of GarsideHomology; that of Dehornoy–Lafont and Garnier is obtained by opposite.
  • Mutability: a query may update the memo of a lazy germ, so that concurrent queries are only safe for a germ documented as immutable.

Interface

Required for every germ: natoms, length(G, s) (a method of Base.length, ℓ(s)), product, ldiv, left_descents, lcm_atom, opposite. Required for a germ whose simples are all numbered (tabulated germ), optional otherwise: nsimples (the number of simples, a constant, also defined by a lazy germ that knows it without enumerating) and the internal method GarsideHomology._simple_numbers(G), the range Int32(0):Int32(nsimples(G) - 1), from which simples and elements are derived. Derived, with a generic method that a germ may replace: one(G), isone(G, s), atoms, is_left_divisor, word, isless_lex, isless_deglex.

Arguments that are simples accept any integer; simples are returned as Int32, lengths and counts as Int. For the user, a germ is also callable and gives its elements as GermElements, written as words in the atoms: G(1, 2, 1), G("121"), G().

GarsideHomology.lcm_atom — Function
lcm_atom(G::AbstractGerm, ω, a) -> NamedTuple{(:lcm, :complement), NTuple{2, Int32}}

The right lcm ω ∨ a of the simple ω and the atom a, and its complement τ, such that ωτ = ω ∨ a: (lcm = ω ∨ a, complement = τ), which destructures as ωa, τ = lcm_atom(G, ω, a). If a ≼ ω, the result is (lcm = ω, complement = 0).

GarsideHomology.opposite — Function
opposite(G::AbstractGerm)

The germ of the opposite monoid Mᵒᵖ: the same atoms, in the same order, and the reversed product, s ·ᵒᵖ t = ts. Its left divisibility is the right divisibility of M, its lcms are the left lcms of M. A tabulated opposite numbers its simples in its own canonical order, so that opposite(opposite(G)) numbers them as G.

GarsideHomology.word — Function

The smallest reduced word of the simple s in the atoms, for the lexicographic order: at each step, the smallest left descent. Empty for the identity.

The smallest reduced word of s in the atoms of its germ, for the lexicographic order: word(s.germ, s.index). Empty for the identity.

GarsideHomology.isless_lex — Function
isless_lex(G::AbstractGerm, s, t) -> Bool

Whether the smallest word of s comes strictly before that of t in the lexicographic order, a prefix coming first. The generic method compares the words letter by letter, without allocating; a tabulated germ compares precomputed ranks.

source
GarsideHomology.isless_deglex — Function
isless_deglex(G::AbstractGerm, s, t) -> Bool

Whether s comes strictly before t in the degree-lexicographic order: the shorter first, then by smallest words in the lexicographic order (isless_lex). It is the canonical order of the simples of a tabulated germ, whose method compares their numbers.

Elements

GarsideHomology.GermElement — Type
GermElement

An element of a germ G (a simple), for the user: the germ and the number of the simple. It is written as a word in the atoms, following the settings of the display (Notation): 121 for B⁺(A₂), e for the identity, 1·12·3 in a germ with at least 10 atoms.

Built by calling the germ:

  • G(), the identity; G(a), the atom a; G(1, 2, 1), the product of the atoms of a word, an ArgumentError if it is not a simple;
  • G("121"), read from its written form, with the settings of the session; spaces, ·, ⋅ and . between the atoms are accepted, and parentheses around the word.

Also given by simples(G) and elements(G), and by the accessors of the cells and of the elements of free modules. Operations: word(s), the smallest word in the atoms; length(s); isone(s), one(s); s * t, the product if it is a simple (an ArgumentError otherwise: the products of the monoid, by normal forms, are not supported yet); s \ t, the left quotient if s ≼ t; equality, hashing and the degree-lexicographic order (isless). A GermElement is a scalar for broadcasting.

GermElement(G, s) wraps the number s of a simple of G (checked against the simples numbered by G): a tool of the developer.

Construction

GarsideHomology.germ — Function
germ(M)
germ(W)

The germ of the monoid M, of the type best suited to its size. Methods are added by the package extensions: with Chevie loaded, M may be a BraidMonoid of a finite Coxeter group or a DualBraidMonoid (of a Coxeter group, or of a well-generated irreducible complex reflection group), and the result is a TableGerm if the number of simples, computed from the degrees, is at most 5·10⁵. Above, a braid monoid gets a lazy CoxeterGerm, and a dual braid monoid an ArgumentError (there is no lazy germ for them). TableGerm(M) forces the tabulation, CoxeterGerm(cartan(W)) the lazy germ.

For a finite Coxeter group W of Chevie (coxgroup, coxsym, and the groups that crg builds as Coxeter groups, such as crg(23), which is coxgroup(:H, 3)), germ(W) is the germ of its classical braid monoid, germ(BraidMonoid(W)); for another reflection group of Chevie (a PermRootGroup, such as crg(4)), an ArgumentError, the dual braid monoid being given by dual_germ.

source
GarsideHomology.dual_germ — Function
dual_germ(W; c = 1:n)

The germ of the dual braid monoid of the well-generated irreducible reflection group W for the Coxeter element c, given as a word in the generators of W. By default, c is their product in order, 1:n, if it is a regular element of order h (Springer's criterion: its eigenspace for e^{2iπ/h} is a line outside the reflecting hyperplanes); otherwise, the first order of the generators that is, in the lexicographic order, with a message naming it (for G₂₉: [1, 2, 4, 3]); an ArgumentError if there is none. The germ depends on c up to isomorphism. The monoid itself is not built: for a real group (2 among the degrees), the simples are found by the criterion of Brady and Watt; for a complex group, by the Hurwitz orbit of a reduced decomposition of c (hurwitz_orbit). Methods are added by the package extensions (Chevie): W may be a finite Coxeter group (coxgroup) or a complex reflection group (crg). The same threshold as for germ applies. For a large complex group, building DualBraidMonoid(W) itself with Chevie takes minutes (about 4 for G₃₄), where dual_germ(W) takes a fraction of a second.

source
GarsideHomology.TableGerm — Type
TableGerm{K} <: AbstractGerm

A germ all of whose simples are numbered in the canonical order (identity 0, atoms 1:N, then by length and smallest word), with dense tables of size N × n (N atoms, n simples): the right covers sa, the left quotients a \ s by the atoms and the lcms s ∨ a with their complements; the left descents (a StaticBitSet{K}), the lengths and the lexicographic ranks of the smallest words. Products and left divisions of any two simples are computed along the smallest word of one of them, in ℓ steps, with direct answers for the identity and the atoms. The memory is linear in n·N: about 16 bytes per simple and atom, 49 MB for B*(E₈).

Built by tabulate or TableGerm(R, identity) from the right covers, or by TableGerm(G) from any germ whose simples are all numbered. The tables are written only by the constructor, so that a TableGerm can be read by several threads at once.

source
GarsideHomology.CoxeterGerm — Type
CoxeterGerm{n} <: AbstractGerm
CoxeterGerm(C)

The germ of the classical braid monoid B⁺(W) of the finite Coxeter group W of rank n given by its integral Cartan matrix C (crystallographic types), the simples being the elements of W. The germ is lazy: its simples are numbered as they are met (identity 0, atoms 1:n in the order of the rows of C, the others in the order of the queries, which is not canonical), for the large groups that cannot be tabulated, such as E₈ (696 729 600 simples, of which the construction of the resolution meets a few percent).

An element w is identified by its key, the indices of the roots w(α₁), …, w(αₙ), with the convention of Chevie for the product (σ·τ acts as σ then τ on the indices of the roots). For each simple met, only its key and its length are kept; left descents are read on the key, and the other operations computed with the coordinates of the roots in the basis of the simple roots. The lcms with the atoms are memorised. A query numbers only the simple it returns: divisions, words and lexicographic comparisons follow the smallest words on the keys, without numbering the intermediate quotients.

nsimples is |W|, from the exponents (Kostant: heights of the positive roots); nnumbered is the number of simples numbered so far; simples is not defined. A query may number new simples: a CoxeterGerm must not be queried from several threads at once. Two CoxeterGerm are equal only if identical, their numberings depending on the history of their queries. C and its transpose give the same germ (the Weyl groups of dual root systems are the same Coxeter group).

source
GarsideHomology.hurwitz_orbit — Function
hurwitz_orbit(start::NTuple{n, <:Integer}, conjugate; limit = 10^8) -> Vector

The orbit of the tuple start under the Hurwitz action of the braid group on n strands: the generator σₖ replaces the entries (x, y) at the positions k, k + 1 by (x y x⁻¹, x), which preserves the product of the tuple. The entries are indices of group elements, and conjugate(i, j) must return the index of xᵢ xⱼ xᵢ⁻¹.

The orbit is computed by a breadth-first search with the moves σₖ only: this suffices for a finite orbit, on which each σₖ acts as a permutation. It is returned in the order of discovery, start first; an ArgumentError is thrown if it exceeds limit tuples. For a Coxeter element c of a well-generated complex reflection group and a reduced decomposition of c into reflections, the orbit is the set of all reduced decompositions of c (Bessis), of size n!·hⁿ/|W|.

Checks

GarsideHomology.verify — Function
verify(G::AbstractGerm; full = false)

Check the consistency of the tables of a germ whose simples are all numbered (a tabulated germ, a germ of the tests, a germ read from a file), and throw an ArgumentError naming the first defect. Checked, in O(n·N) operations of the interface:

  • the identity and the atoms (lengths 0 and 1, words, descents);
  • the left descents of each simple are exactly the atoms that divide it, with quotients of length one less;
  • each simple product by an atom adds 1 to the length and divides back;
  • for each simple ω and atom a, lcm_atom gives a common multiple ω·τ = ω ∨ a, equal to (ω, 1) when a ≼ ω;
  • a single simple Δ without right cover, which every simple divides;
  • the canonical order (by length, then smallest word) and the lexicographic comparison.

With full = true, also the lattice property, in O(#intervals · N): each ω ∨ a divides every common multiple of ω and a.

source
GarsideHomology.count_multichains — Function
count_multichains(G::AbstractGerm, m) -> Int

The number of multichains σ₁ ≼ σ₂ ≼ ⋯ ≼ σₘ of m simples of G (repetitions allowed), whose simples must all be numbered: the number of simples for m = 1, of pairs σ ≼ ν for m = 2. For the dual braid monoid of a well-generated group, this is the Fuss–Catalan number Π (dᵢ + m·h)/dᵢ. Computed by m − 1 passes over the intervals, in O(n) memory; an OverflowError is thrown rather than a wrong count.

GarsideHomology.GermFingerprint — Type
GermFingerprint

An invariant of isomorphism of germs, independent of the numberings of the simples and of the atoms (J1.c, c6), given by fingerprint: the number of atoms, the length of Δ, the number of simples of each length, the numbers of multichains for m = 2 and 3 (see count_multichains), the profile of the descents (descent_profile[ℓ + 1, d + 1] simples of length ℓ with d left descents) and the Möbius invariant μ(1, Δ). Two fingerprints compare by ==. Not a complete invariant, but enough to detect an error of construction or of convention between two presentations of a germ.

GarsideHomology.germ_isomorphism — Function
germ_isomorphism(G::AbstractGerm, H::AbstractGerm; limit = 10^6)
    -> Union{Vector{Int32}, Nothing}

An isomorphism of germs from G to H, given by the images of the atoms: φ[a] is the atom of H image of the atom a of G; nothing when the germs are not isomorphic. An isomorphism is a bijection of the atoms that extends to a bijection of the simples with φ(σ·a) = φ(σ)·φ(a), the product being defined in G exactly when it is defined in H; it then preserves the lengths, the divisibility, the lcms and Δ.

The search is a backtracking on the atoms of G, taken by decreasing number of defined products a·b of two atoms (the atom of smaller number first on a tie), an atom being sent only to an atom of H with the same numbers of defined products on the right and on the left. The images of the products of two atoms already placed are kept consistent along the way, which imposes the relations ab = cd. Once all the atoms are placed, the map is extended to the simples along the right covers, from the identity, and checked on all of them. limit bounds the number of trials of an image of an atom: an ArgumentError is thrown when it is reached, so that nothing always means that the germs are not isomorphic.

Germs with different numbers of atoms or of simples are told apart without search. Every simple of G and H is visited by products with the atoms: for a lazy germ such as CoxeterGerm, all its simples get numbered.

For developers

GarsideHomology.StaticBitSet — Type
StaticBitSet{K}

Set of integers of 1:64K, stored as K words of 64 bits: the integer i is the bit i - 1. The value is immutable and isbits, so that sets are stored inline in arrays and returned without allocation; K is a constant of the type, fixed for instance by a germ from its number of atoms (K = cld(N, 64)).

StaticBitSet{K}() is the empty set and StaticBitSet{K}(itr) the set of the integers of itr, each in 1:64K. Fast methods: in, iteration in increasing order, length, isempty, first, issubset, union, intersect, setdiff, symdiff, isdisjoint, ==. As an AbstractSet{Int}, a StaticBitSet also has the generic methods of sets (collect, comparison and union with other sets, hash).

GarsideHomology.tabulate — Function
tabulate(R, identity) -> (G, renumber)

The TableGerm G of the germ given by its right covers, and the numbering of its simples. The simples are numbered 1:n in any order (a provisional numbering), identity being the identity; R is an N × n matrix of integers whose entry R[a, σ] is the simple σa if it is simple, 0 otherwise, so that the atoms are the R[a, identity], in the order of the rows. renumber[p] is the number in G of the simple numbered p in R; the inverse numbering is sortperm(renumber).

The construction checks (and throws an ArgumentError naming the provisional simples otherwise): the entries and the atoms; that every simple is reached from the identity; homogeneity (each cover adds 1 to the length, lengths at most 255); right and left cancellation; associativity; that the germ is bounded (a single simple without right cover); that each simple has a common multiple with each atom, the smallest of which is unique among the common multiples of its length. The full lattice property and the numbers of simples and of intervals are checked separately.

source
tabulate(G::AbstractGerm) -> (T, renumber)

The TableGerm T of a germ G whose simples are all numbered (simples(G) is defined), built from its right covers, and the numbering: renumber[s + 1] is the number in T of the simple s of G (the identity if G is in the canonical order).

Resolutions, cells and elements

This page works in the reduced lcm resolution of the classical braid monoid B⁺(A₂), which has one cell of degree 0, two of degree 1 and one of degree 2. Its outputs are those of the tests of the package.

using GarsideHomology, Chevie

A2 = germ(coxgroup(:A, 2))       # simples e, 1, 2, 12, 21, 121
P = lcm_resolution(A2)

Elements of a germ

A germ is called to give its elements, as GermElements: A2(1, 2) is the product of the atoms 1 and 2, A2("12") the same simple read from its written form, A2() the identity, and simples(A2) the list of the simples. The product s * t is defined when it is a simple (A2(1) * A2(2) == A2("12"); A2(1) * A2(1) is an ArgumentError), and s \ t is the left quotient when s ≼ t. Also word(s), the smallest word of s in the atoms, and length(s).

Cells

The cells of degree n are the basis of the module P[n]: basis(P[1]) holds the cells 1 and 2, and basis(P) all the cells, degree by degree. A cell is also given by its bar form, x = P("2|12"), or by its letters, P(A2(2), A2(1, 2)); P() is the cell of degree 0. A Cell reads as the sequence of its letters (x[1], x[end], length(x)), with its weight cell_weight(x), here 121, its degree cell_degree(x) and its name in the snapshots, atom_sequence(x).

At the REPL, the resolution shows its cells and boundaries (abridged here):

julia> P
LCMResolution: 4 cells, by degree [1, 2, 1], …
Cells:
  0: e
  1: 1, 2
  2: 2|12
Differential:
  (boundaries of degree 1 omitted, such as d(1) = 1[] - []1)
  d(2|12) = …

show(stdout, "text/plain", P), or the same into a file, writes all the boundaries.

Elements of the modules

The elements of the modules are sums of terms c×σ[x]τ: a cell x, an integer c and simples σ and τ acting on the two sides (BasedFreeModuleElement). They are built from cells, read from their written form, and combined:

y = P(P("1"))                    # the generator [1]
P("2×1[2]1 - [1]")               # written -[1] + 2×1[2]1
A2(1) * P(P("2")) == P("1[2]")   # the action on the left
y * A2(2) == P("[1]2")           # and on the right

The identity is omitted on both sides, the coefficients ±1 too, and the cell of degree 0 is written []. The marker × separates a coefficient from what follows when the atoms are written with digits. v[σ, x, τ] is the coefficient of a term, and the iteration over v gives the terms as tuples (c, σ, x, τ).

The differential

d = differential(P)
d(P("1"))                        # 1[] - []1
d(A2(2) * y) == P("21[] - 2[]1")
iszero(d(d(x)))                  # true

d(x) is the boundary of the generator x, and d(v) that of an element (Differential). The matrices of the differential are differential(P, n), from degree n + 1 to degree n: LabelledSparseMatrixes whose entries carry the pairs (σ, τ).

Extensions of scalars

E = extend_scalars(P) is the complex over ℤ obtained by letting the simples act by 1 on both sides, ℤ ⊗ P ⊗ ℤ; its bases are the same cells, and its matrices are SparseMatrixCSCs. Its elements are written without simples (E(P("1")) is [1]), and Vector(differential(E)(x)) gives the column of x. The other tensor products, with ℤε for instance, are on the page Coefficients and homology.

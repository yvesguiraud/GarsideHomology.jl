Coefficients and homology

Modules of coefficients

A module of coefficients is a module over the monoid algebra, free of finite rank over ℤ (AbstractMonoidModule). Two are defined for now:

  • TrivialModule(), in which every element of the monoid acts by 1;
  • SignModule(), also written ℤε, in which every atom acts by −1, so that a simple s acts by (−1)^ℓ(s).

A ring stands for its trivial module: homology(R, ℤ) is homology(R, TrivialModule()).

Homology

For a resolution R, homology(R, M) is the homology of the monoid, hence of its group of fractions, with coefficients in M, in all degrees; homology(R, M, n) in degree n. The result is a FinitelyGeneratedGradedAbelianGroup: H[n] is the FinitelyGeneratedAbelianGroup of degree n, with its free rank rank(H[n]) and its invariant factors torsion(H[n]). A group is written torsion first, (ℤ/3ℤ)² ⊕ ℤ/6ℤ ⊕ ℤ, and this written form is read back by parse(FinitelyGeneratedAbelianGroup, s), which also accepts Z for ℤ, ℤ/2 for ℤ/2ℤ, ^k for an exponent and + for ⊕.

The elementary divisors are computed by SparseElementaryDivisors.jl, and the keyword arguments of homology go to it: value_type, the integer types tried during the elimination, nworkers, verbose, and the others described in its documentation.

Tensor products

homology(R, M) is the homology of the complex ℤ ⊗ R ⊗ M over ℤ, which tensor_product and ⊗ give directly:

using GarsideHomology: ⊗           # or with Chevie loaded

C = ℤ ⊗ R ⊗ ℤε                     # the complex over ℤ
L = R ⊗ ℤ                          # free left modules: the terms keep their σ
N = ℤ ⊗ R                          # free right modules: the terms keep their τ

ℤ ⊗ R ⊗ ℤ is extend_scalars(R). The results share the bases of R, whose elements are still cells, so that their elements are written and read as in R.

Complexes over ℤ

The generic layer, GarsideHomology.Complexes, also computes the homology of complexes of integer matrices, given by their differentials d_0, d_1, … with d_n : C_{n+1} → C_n:

using GarsideHomology, SparseArrays

# Cellular chain complex of the real projective plane: d₀ = 0, d₁ = 2.
C = ChainComplex([spzeros(Int, 1, 1), sparse([1], [1], [2], 1, 1)])
homology(C)                        # ℤ, ℤ/2ℤ, 0 in degrees 0 to 2
is_square_zero(C)                  # true

collapsible_pairs lists the pairs of basis elements joined by a coefficient ±1 in a differential, for a complex over ℤ or over a monoid algebra.
